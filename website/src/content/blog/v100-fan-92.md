A local coding agent does not keep its GPU busy continuously. It generates a response, calls a tool, waits for the result, and generates again. That makes its cooling requirements different from those of a single uninterrupted benchmark.

We tested a Tesla V100 PCIe 32 GB with a 92 mm fan and a 3D-printed adapter under two agent workloads: a short documentation task and longer article generation with requested pauses. We tracked GPU and HBM temperatures, power, fan speed, and inference throughput to describe how this particular setup behaved.

## Hardware and cooling setup

- GPU: NVIDIA Tesla V100 PCIe, 32 GB.
- Fan: **ARCTIC P9 Max**, 92 mm.
- Adapter: [NVIDIA Tesla P40/V100 funnel for a 92 mm fan](https://www.printables.com/model/1839179-nvidia-tesla-p40-v100-funnel-for-92mm-fan), a 3D-printed duct coupling the fan to the card.
- Model: `qwen3.8-27b@iq4_xs`, served by LM Studio.
- Agent harness, workload orchestration, and tool execution: [skiller.run](https://skiller.run).

The V100's passive heatsink depends on external airflow. The adapter channels air from the P9 Max into the heatsink, while the BMC controls fan speed. The results therefore describe the adapter, fan, and control profile together—not the fan running at its maximum speed.

## Monitoring methodology

The [skiller.run](https://skiller.run) monitor agent orchestrates the experiment: it runs the setup scripts, waits for a task, and starts sampling before executing it. Sampling continues through generation, tool calls, and pauses. Once the agent delivers its final response, the workflow stops sampling, collects the results, and waits for the next task.

We collect three independent data streams:

- **GPU telemetry:** GPU temperature, HBM temperature, and GPU power draw through `nvidia-smi`.
- **Fan telemetry:** FANB speed through the BMC using `ipmitool`.
- **Inference events:** LM Studio input/output events and per-generation statistics, including generated tokens and reported tokens per second.

Sensors target one query per second; actual timestamps are retained. FANB reports the fan connected to the BMC, not a fan sensor on the GPU.

The charts share a time axis starting at the first GPU capture. Because the monitors start and stop sequentially, their coverage differs slightly; missing intervals remain empty. Each generation's tokens/s value is plotted at its LMS output timestamp. It describes that entire generation, not instantaneous throughput.

Original samples and charts are retained in the experiment repository.

## What the measurements tell us

During the longer capture (**11 min 49 s**), GPU power peaked at **249.87 W** and FANB reached **3,700 RPM**. GPU temperature peaked at **76 °C**, while HBM reached **86 °C**. These are peaks during an intermittent workload, not sustained operating values.

The model generated **16,461 tokens** across 16 generations at **29.71–31.20 tokens/s**, averaging **30.38 tokens/s**. Throughput remained within a narrow band, but speed alone cannot rule out thermal throttling; GPU clocks and throttle reasons were not captured.

Cooling continued after the load subsided. At the end of the capture, power was down to **47.67 W**, but GPU/HBM temperatures were still **63/65 °C**, compared with **56/57 °C** at the start. The recording ended before a return to the initial temperatures was observed.

Separately, we noticed air flowing back near the adapter's connector side, suggesting turbulence or recirculation. This was a qualitative observation, not a measured airflow result. A longer adapter might improve airflow distribution, but we did not test that modification.

## Conclusion

With this adapter, ARCTIC P9 Max, and BMC fan control, the GPU stayed below NVIDIA's **83 °C** operating maximum, but HBM peaked **1 °C above its 85 °C maximum**. The tested cooling configuration did not keep both components within their specified operating temperatures, even though generation remained around **30 tokens/s**.

The practical takeaway is to monitor HBM as well as the GPU core: core temperature and stable throughput alone would have missed this excursion. This result describes the tested configuration, not the fan's maximum cooling capacity or a comparison with another cooler.
